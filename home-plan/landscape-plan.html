Landscape plan and rec pond

A natural swimming pond in the style of Greg Wittstock and Aquascape is the centerpiece of the back yard. There's no chlorine: a planted wetland filters the water and spills back in over a waterfall. The pond runs along the back of the house, so the terrace ends at the water. Around it are a sun ledge, a beach, a fire pit across the water and a loop path, with gardens and a small pond out front. This revision is tuned for Youngsville, Louisiana: zone 9a plants, the water table, heavy rain, long summers and an open, flat property.

The far shore is for show: the wetland garden runs along the back as its upper shore, with the falls on the axis and a stone lip sheeting water in along the right half. How the water stays clean

This is the Aquascape recipe: an ecosystem, not a pool. Pumps pull water from the intake bay in the far-left corner and push it under the wetland along the far shore. It rises up through the gravel, where bacteria and plant roots strip out the nutrients algae feed on. Then it pours back in over the waterfall and sheets over the wetland's stone lip along the right half. Water enters across the far shore and leaves at the far-left corner, so the whole lap lane moves, right to left. Frogs and dragonflies will show up, and the water gets better with age. Expect a year or two before the wetland fills in.

Building it in Youngsville

Foundation
With the water this close, the porch footings set how near the deep water can come. The 5 ft zone stays outside a 45° line down from the porch footing (red dashed line in the section). A structural engineer should design the porch slab, the coping and the pond edge together, with drainage away from the slab.
Water table
South Louisiana's groundwater sits high, and a pond liner with water under it can float up in "whales." Plan on a sub-liner drain with a sump and a vent, and get a soil boring before digging. The clay-heavy soil holds the shape well once it's drained.
Rain and flooding
The rim sits a few inches above the surrounding grade, with a shallow swale around it, so lawn runoff and fertilizer stay out of the pond. The overflow is sized for 10 inch rain events. Check the FEMA flood zone for the pond site as well as the house.
Heat
Summer water will reach the upper 80s. The 8 ft deep zone stays cooler, aeration runs all summer, and the falls and lip add oxygen. Shade from the maples and live oaks on the afternoon side helps once north is known. Tropical water lilies love it.
Year-round
There's no winter shutdown, so the pumps run 12 months and the growing season, and the algae season, are long. The wetland and IonGen carry that load.
Mosquitoes and wildlife
Moving water and fish keep mosquitoes out, so fish are close to a must here. Cottonmouths visit any Louisiana pond with frogs, so keep the beach and step edges clear of tall plants.
Hurricanes
Put the pumps and aeration on the house generator, because stagnant warm water turns fast without power. Plan to skim storm debris and bring in the ledge and terrace furniture.
Stone
South Louisiana has no native rock, so every boulder and all the gravel come in by truck, usually from Arkansas, Oklahoma or the Texas Hill Country. Pick one stone type, such as Arkansas fieldstone or Texas moss rock, so it reads as natural.

Rough budget

Rough, checked against real prices on Oct 26, 2026. A Texas builder starts a 50' × 70' swim pond with wetland at $130,000, including the dig, liner and equipment. Cost guides put natural pools at $50–$100 per sq ft, cheaper per foot as they get bigger. This pond plus its wetland is about 3,400 sq ft, so it lands around $130k–$210k once you add boulders, falls, coping, lights and trucking stone into south Louisiana. The pumps, vault and design don't grow with the size; mostly the dig, liner and stone do. Rev. B's $175k–$260k scaled up a small pond's starting price, which overcounted the fixed setup. Flagstone runs about $15–$30 per sq ft installed, and landscape lights about $100–$150 each. The planting, gardens and front pond lines haven't been checked against outside prices yet.

Open questions

  1. Fish. Koi, native fish like bluegill and mosquitofish, or both? Some fish are close to a must here for mosquitoes.
  2. North. It decides where afternoon shade falls and whether the fire pit should swap sides.
  3. Pool rules. Louisiana's statewide code points to the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code, which asks for a 4 ft barrier around water over 24 in deep. Whether a natural pond counts is the City of Youngsville's call, so ask the permit office early. With the water this close to the porch, the house's back wall becomes part of the barrier, which usually means alarms or self-closing, self-latching hardware on the glass wall, porch door and dining doors. The dashed line shows the fence that closes the rest, with gates.
  4. Flood zone. Do you know the FEMA zone for the back of the property? It affects how high the pond rim and the wetland sit.
