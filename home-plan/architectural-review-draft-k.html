Architectural review before the 3D model

The floor plan holds up. Every room is closed in, nothing is missing on the main floor, and almost every door and aisle is the right size. The roof and the bonus room don't work yet. The placeholder roof has two flat valleys and a wing that drains into a wall. The stair also comes up where there's only 3 to 5 ft of headroom.

Bottom lineEverything below the ceiling line can go into the detailed model as drawn. The roof and the stair-to-bonus section need an architect's roof plan first, or the model will be built on a roof that leaks and a bonus room you can't stand up in.

What checks out

Walls
Every room on the main floor is closed where it should be and open where it should be. I traced each wall line in the plan data and found no gaps. One 5' × 2' spot at the primary bedroom door has no label. It works as a small vestibule off the hall, so just name it.
Main axis
The front doors, the 16 ft glass wall and the waterfall all sit on x 37.5. The back porch posts at 29.5 and 45.5 frame the glass exactly, so nothing blocks the view.
Kitchen
The island-to-range aisle is about 51 in, and the island-to-sink aisle is about 56 in. Both beat the 48 in target. The fridge and range are on the stair wall, clear of the hall opening.
Halls
The main hall clears about 3'8". The bedroom hall clears about 3'0", which is the code minimum and fine for a short run.
Bedrooms
Every bedroom has a window big enough for emergency escape. The primary bedroom also has the slider. Bedroom 3 is 12'4" deep now, which fixes the problem in the earlier review.
Baths
The toilets have the required side and front clearances, including the tight powder room (18 in from the toilet's center to the wall).
Pond
The water's closest point to the house is about 10 ft, at bedroom 2. That works with the sub-liner drain already in the pond spec.

Roofline

Short answer: no, it doesn't make sense yet. The roof in the 3D model was always a placeholder: a separate gable over each wing. That gives seven gables at four different wall heights (10, 11, 12 and 15 ft), with ridges from 17'6" to 26'. Where those gables meet, several joints can't shed water. None of this is a floor plan problem. It's the next design step, and it should happen before anyone spends money on a detailed model.

A direction for the architect. Let the great room carry the main side-gabled roof, since it's the tallest and most important room. Treat the bedroom wing, the lounge and the primary suite as front-to-back gables that meet it in proper valleys or hips. Put the office and primary under one roof, or join them with a low connector so no two eaves run side by side. Make the garage either a front-facing gable or a side gable with a long shed dormer on the back. Either way, it must give the bonus room real headroom (next section). That keeps the modern farmhouse look from your wife's photos and gets rid of every flat valley.

Stairs and bonus room

HeadroomThe stair arrives at the back of the house (y 38 to 40). That's the low edge of the great room, office and garage roofs, so there's only 3 to 5 ft over the top step. Code needs 6'8". The bonus room's entry opening (x 78.5, y 40.6 to 44.6) has the same problem. Option 2's whole back hall sits under the low eave too, with 2'6" to 5'10" of headroom. Fix: a shed dormer across the back of the garage roof over the stair hall, or taller garage walls, or a stair that arrives toward the front.
Riser countThe bonus floor sits about 12'6" above the main floor. The plan draws two runs of 8 treads, which makes 18 risers of about 8.3 in each. The code limit is 7¾ in. You need 20 risers: two runs of 9 treads at 10 in, plus the landing. That's still about 11'4" long, so it fits in the same 11'6" stair box. The 3D model draws 9 treads per run (19 risers, about 7.9 in), which is close but still over the limit.
Top landingThe second run ends 5 in from the hall wall, so the top step leads straight into a wall. Code needs a 36 in landing in the direction you're walking. End the run about 3 ft sooner, or turn the top onto the landing strip over the closet.
Upstairs windowsBoth upstairs options draw windows that can't exist. The ones on the back wall (y 40) sit in a 2'6" knee wall with the office and primary roofs right behind it. The ones on the left wall (x 78.5, y 53 to 58) open into the great room attic. Only the gable-end window and the two front dormers get daylight. A bedroom up there needs an escape window, so this matters if option 2 is chosen.
Floor depthThe bonus floor spans about 26 to 29 ft over the garage with no posts. That takes about 16 in deep joists or trusses, plus the subfloor. The 3D model allows about 11 in. Either drop the garage slab a step or plan on a 13 ft floor-to-floor and 21 risers.

Your wife is redrawing the stairs anyway. The numbers to give her: about 20 risers at 7½ in, about 16 ft of total tread run, a 36 in landing at the top, and an arrival point under the middle of the garage roof, not its back edge.

Structure to hand the engineer

Glass wall
A 16 ft header, plus a pocket wall about 12 in thick to hold four stacked panels. A normal exterior wall is 6 in, so the pocket from x 25.1 to 29.5 needs to be drawn thicker.
Kitchen–dining
The great room's back wall line (y 34) has no wall for 16 ft where the kitchen opens to the dining room. A beam has to carry the roof there.
Garage front
A 21 ft header over the 10 ft door that also carries the bonus room's front wall and roof.
Bonus floor
A 26 to 29 ft clear span with no posts (see above). It also has an unsupported corner over the trash area (roof problem 5).
Porch posts
The back porch has a 16 ft span between the posts at 29.5 and 45.5 (needs a beam). The middle post was moved, as already noted in the handoff.

Code and safety items

Based on the residential code (IRC) and electrical code (NEC) most Louisiana parishes use. Confirm locally with the Youngsville or Lafayette Parish building office.

Grocery hatch
The code allows only rated doors between a garage and the house, so an open hatch or a window there isn't allowed. Use a small 20-minute rated, self-closing pass-through door. The 3D model currently shows a glass window.
Garage door
The garage-to-hall door needs to be solid or fire-rated, ideally self-closing. If the garage floor is a step down, it needs a 3 ft landing on the garage side. The truck's tailgate leaves about 3'7".
Range hood
A pro range hood over 400 CFM needs a makeup-air system. The hood's duct goes up through the 11 ft ceiling and out through the roof, because the range is on an inside wall.
Generator
The pad sits about 5 in from the garage wall. Generac wants at least 18 in from the house and 5 ft from any opening window or door. Slide it out about 1'6".
Shower windows
The windows in bath 2's tub/shower, bath 3's shower and the primary shower need tempered glass and a waterproof sill, or they can move.
Panels
The panels in the server room need a 30 in wide by 36 in deep clear space in front, so keep shelving out of it. With the panels inside, the meter needs an outdoor main or emergency disconnect, which also suits the generator transfer switch.
Attic access
HVAC and the water heater in the attic need a code-sized access opening and a walkway. A knee-wall door from the upstairs stair hall into the great room attic is the easiest way.
Wind
The glass wall, the 21 ft garage door and the windows need design-pressure ratings for the local hurricane wind speed (about 140 to 150 mph in Lafayette Parish). Impact glass is worth pricing for the glass wall even if it's not required.
Pond barrier
If the city treats the pond as a pool, the glass wall, porch door, dining doors and primary slider become part of the barrier and need alarms or self-closing hardware. This is already an open question in the handoff.

Smaller catches

Front porch posts
The middle pair sits at 33.7 and 42.3. That centers them on x 38, so they're 6 in off the front door axis. Move them to 33.2 and 41.8 so the door is centered between them.
Dressing room
The full-length mirror on the end wall is drawn right over the window (x 106, y 10 to 13). Move the window or the mirror. The wardrobes are drawn 19 in deep, and hanging clothes need 24 in. The bench sits in the aisle, which breaks the "nothing in walkways" rule.
Office vestibule
The office's sound boundary includes the corridor door, so that door and the hall door both need seals and solid cores.
Outdoor kitchen
The grill sits under a covered roof, so it needs a rated vent hood and clearance from the beadboard ceiling.

Where the 3D model differs from the plan

Roof
The roof exists only in the 3D model. The floor plan has no roof plan, so every roof issue above comes from the model's placeholder roof.
Stairs
The plan has 8 treads per run, and the model has 9.
Bonus floor
The model uses about 11 in of floor depth. A real floor needs about 16 to 18 in.
Hatch
The plan draws a hatch, and the model shows a glass window.
Upstairs windows
The bonus-room windows on the upstairs plans aren't in the model, and most of them couldn't be built (see above).
Garage end wall
The model has a hole above the trash area.

Recommended order

  1. 1Fix the six small plan items: the bedroom wing opening, the grocery hatch, the bedroom 3 and server doors, the porch posts, and the dressing room mirror and window. The 3D model can pick these up as is.
  2. 2Have an architect or experienced designer draw a real roof plan and a section through the stair and bonus room, using the direction above. This is the one thing that should come before the detailed model.
  3. 3Your wife redraws the stair with 20 risers and a top landing, arriving under the middle of the garage roof.
  4. 4Then build the full 3D model and renders.
