Four pond shapes to compare

Each option keeps what works in the current plan: the terrace, the living room view, a way in for kids and adults, a wetland filter at least 30% of the water, external pumps at the near end and the falls at the far end. What changes is the shape and the mood. All are drawn at the same scale as the house.

The current shape

For comparison: the round pond from the landscape plan.

Options

A. Island lagoon

The biggest and most natural: a wide, scalloped lagoon around a planted boulder island with a small maple. You swim laps around the island. The beach, steps and deck stay on the near shore, the falls sit back-left over the deep end, and a long stream comes down the right side.

B. Bayou bend

The most Louisiana: a crescent of water wraps a fire-pit peninsula off the terrace, so you sit with water on three sides. Bald cypress and a boardwalk across the left arm give it a bayou feel. Swim the whole arc; the falls and deep end are at the back of the bend.

C. Quarry

The least round: crisp edges of big flat stone slabs, like an old swimming hole in a quarry. A wide sheet waterfall pours off a stacked stone wall, a high flat rock at the back right is the cliff jump, and a shallow stone sun ledge sits in the front corner.

D. Two-pool cascade

A long, diagonal swim pond with a smaller upper soaking pool tucked at the back left. The wetland feeds the upper pool, which spills over a rock dam into the deep end. A stream comes down the right. The upper pool could be heated later as a natural hot tub.

Cost
Cost follows water area and stone. The quarry uses the most cut slab and costs the most per square foot. The island lagoon and bayou bend are the most water. The cascade adds a second pool and a second falls.
Mix and match
The pieces trade freely. The bayou's fire-pit peninsula, the quarry's sheet falls, or the cascade's upper soaking pool can go on any of the other shapes.
