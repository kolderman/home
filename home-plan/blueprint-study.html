Bottom linePick primary option B and great room option G2. Together they add under 200 sf and change no roof shapes, and they fix the two things you named.

What's already strong, and what holds it back

Strong. The front doors line up with the 16 ft glass wall and the pond. Living and kitchen are one open room about 44 ft by 28'6". There's a real scullery, a sound-isolated office, and a primary suite at the quiet end of the house with its own porch. None of that needs to move.

Holding it back.

  • The closet is a corridor. The dressing room is 20 ft by 8 ft, and the walk-through strip uses part of it. That leaves a 3'7" aisle between two wardrobe runs, with no island and one small window.
  • The bath is a strip. It's 210 sf, but only 10'6" deep. The toilet, tub and shower are lined up against the back wall, the sinks face them 3'7" away, and you reach it through the closet. Bath, closet and bedroom are three full-width bands stacked front to back. That layout is why it feels tight, and adding area alone wouldn't fix it.
  • The great room is wide but low. Its ceilings are flat at 12 and 11 ft over a 28'6" span. About 9 ft of height sits unused in the attic right above it.
  • The walk from the front door is blocked. Chairs, the coffee table and a 10'6" sofa with its back to the pond sit in the way.
  • The kitchen notch. A 16 ft by 3'6" notch at the kitchen front leaves the sink window looking into a dark recess.

What G2 does

  • The ceiling goes up into the roof. The living room ceiling rises from 12 ft at the walls to about 20'6" at a glulam ridge beam. Three cypress collar-tie trusses line up with the glass wall's edges and the front-door axis. Sloped cypress beams fill in between them, so your beams still come every 4 ft and still run to the front doors.
  • The kitchen stays at 11 ft. A timber portal at its edge makes the step in height look intended rather than accidental.
  • A clear 6 ft walk from the front doors to the glass. The furniture splits into a TV group and a view group facing the pond, on either side of that walk.
  • A cross line under the ridge. The range, the 13'6" island and the TV all center on it, with a chandelier where it crosses the front-door axis. The range stays on the stair wall behind the island.
  • The notch is filled. The kitchen front wall moves out 3'6", which makes the kitchen 16 × 28'6", and the sink moves to a new 10 ft window that looks at the front water feature. That feature shifts forward about 3 ft.
  • Wider front windows. The living room's front windows go from 6 ft to 7 ft.

What it costs. This is a rough, unverified estimate of $45-80k more than G1, mostly for the ridge beam, the trusses and the engineering. Ducts can no longer cross the living room, so plan on two air handlers (likely needed at this size anyway). The taller room will sound livelier, so rugs and an acoustic plaster panel help. The front doors stay at 9 ft unless the porch roof is raised. G3's dormer can be added later, once north is known and you've seen the vault in 3D.
