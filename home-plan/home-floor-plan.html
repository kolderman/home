Floor plan, draft M

A single-level home about 113 feet across, with a bonus floor over the garage, in Youngsville, Louisiana. Draft M builds in your blueprint picks and the lead-architect review. The primary bedroom moves onto the pond, 27 ft from the kitchen island with no long hallway. The office moves into the old primary bedroom. The kitchen runs to the front wall, and a clear gallery crosses the living room from the front doors to the glass. The guest wing gets a daylit foyer and two real suites, and the primary bath and closet grow by pushing the back wall out 3½ ft. The garage and the server room are unchanged, and the living room keeps its flat 12 ft beamed ceiling.

Roof plan

Seen from above, with the front of the house at the bottom. Arrows point downhill. Every slope runs to an eave: no two roofs meet in a level trough, and no roof drains against a wall. A drainage check on a 3-inch grid over the whole roof finds no spot where water can stand.

Great room
One side gable, 8:12, on 12 ft walls, from the bedroom wing to the garage. It's the main roof. Its left gable end stands on the bedroom wing roof, and its right end tucks under the garage roof. The kitchen's front wall now sits under its front eave, so the courtyard beam from draft L goes away.
Bedroom wing
Hipped at the back. Its ridge runs into the great room roof and meets it in two sloped valleys. The back porch roof ties into its side the same way.
Lounge
A steep 10:12 gable facing the street, with the 14 ft window under it. It runs back into the great room roof.
Primary and office
One hip roof over both, 8:12, ridge running front to back into the garage roof. The primary bath's small hip now starts 3½ ft further back, at the new bath wall; it keeps the same pitch and drains the same way.
Office strip
The old side porch roof stays. The office takes in its back 16 ft, so the ceiling there slopes down to about 8 ft at the outer wall. A 16 ft beam at x 106 carries the main roof's eave where the old outside wall comes out.
Garage

Dining and porches
A low 2:12 standing-seam shed over the dining room, and one 1½:12 standing-seam plane over the back porch and the outdoor kitchen, tied in at 11'-6" to clear the glass wall's header.

Changes in this draft

Primary bedroom
Moves to the pond, into the old office spot. You reach it through a small suite foyer right where the hall leaves the kitchen: about 27 ft from the island, with no long hallway. The bedroom is 18' × 22', with a 16 ft glass wall onto a private garden and a small corner window toward the falls. The king bed sits on a solid headboard panel on the foyer wall and faces the glass. The foyer has a bench and a morning bar, and its door now sits off line from the bedroom's pocket door and away from the stair, so no one in the kitchen sees into the bedroom.
Primary bath and closet
A trimmed study option B: the back wall moves out 3½ ft. The closet becomes 20' × 13' (260 sf), with glass-front wardrobes on both long walls, a 7 ft bench in place of an island, a shoe tower, a window seat and a full-length mirror on the end of the front wardrobe. The bath is 20' × 9': a tub under a back window, her vanity on the garden wall under a high clerestory, his vanity on the closet wall, a curbless 6' × 5' shower with a bench, and a private WC. The pocket door from the bedroom straight into the bath is dropped, since it had nowhere to slide; the way in is bedroom, closet, bath.
Office
Moves into the old primary bedroom and takes in the side porch strip: 26' × 16'. Layout 3: a theater lounge at the west end with the OLED on the closet wall, an L couch and a chair, and a workstation at the east end by two windows. All four walls are sound walls. The couch sits about 6" off the door's swing, measured from the face of the double-stud wall.
Kitchen
The courtyard notch is filled, so the kitchen runs to the front wall. The main sink and both dishwashers move to a 15 ft run under a 10 ft window on the front. The range is centered behind a 13'-6" island, and an 18" prep sink in the island's range side cuts the work triangle from about 43 ft to about 24 ft.
Living room
The media wall is centered on the room. A TV group and a group facing the glass sit either side of a clear 6 ft gallery from the front doors to the glass wall. The sofa sits about 8 ft from the screen, which suits a TV of 75" or less. The ceiling stays flat at 12 ft with the cypress beams, as Melissa asked (no cathedral).
Guest wing
The dark 3 ft hall becomes a daylit foyer with a tempered sidelight and a sealed door to the living room. Bedroom 2 gets a vestibule, a 42 sf walk-in, a terrace window, and its own door straight into bath 2. Both baths get 60" vanities. Bath 3's shower grows from 26" (under code) to 36" × 80". The server room gets a sound wall on the bedroom 3 side, surface-mounted panels and a 2'-6" sealed door. The door's swing just touches a corner of the panel clear space, so nothing may be stored there. Bedroom 3's door now swings into the bedroom, so it never meets the living room door in the foyer.
Outdoor kitchen
The leg on the bedroom wall becomes a cold bar only: no grill, ice maker or TV. The grill moves to the west leg.
Doors
The dining French doors swing out, with 2'-8" leaves and a rated sill against wind-driven rain. The powder room gets its own hall door, a 14" shelf cabinet on the bedroom side as a sound buffer, and a quiet exhaust fan (under 0.5 sone) on a timer.
Front water garden
Held at least 3 ft off the new kitchen wall behind a gravel and planting strip, so no water sits against the slab.

Carried over

Glass wall
A 16 ft multi-slide glass wall centered on the front doors, so from the front porch the view runs straight through the house to the pond. Its four panels stack into a pocket on the left. Motorized screens and solar shades drop from ceiling pockets, and the porch door at the right end stays for everyday use.
Lounge
Steel-and-glass double doors to the living room, a flush gas insert on the left wall, and green-lacquer bookcases with a bar cabinet, so it reads as a library.
Kitchen hood
A tapered plaster hood to the ceiling, a marble slab backsplash and a pot filler. Paneled fridge and freezer.
Powder room
A furniture-style sinker cypress vanity with an undermount sink in honed marble, a round brass mirror and a sconce on each side.
Scullery
A third dishwasher, a coffee and beverage station, an appliance garage, a steam or speed oven and open shelves.
Sound walls
Double or staggered studs, mineral wool and two layers of ⅝" drywall with damping compound on all four office walls, the bedroom's dining and outdoor-kitchen wall, the powder room's bedroom wall, the laundry wall and the server room's bedroom 3 wall. The office also gets a sealed solid-core door, a ceiling on resilient channel and lined ducts. No recessed boxes or in-wall speakers in the office walls shared with the suite, and the subwoofer stays off the bedroom wall on isolation feet.

Building specs

Generator
A standby generator on a 3' × 5' pad 18" off the garage's outside wall, in the notch below the mower roll-up door, away from the bedrooms and more than 5 ft from any door or window. An automatic transfer switch at the main disconnect. Size it once the HVAC is picked; 24 to 26 kW air-cooled with load shedding is typical for a house this size.
Garage
One insulated 20' × 10' door so the truck clears, a ductless mini-split head on the back wall, and a Level 2 EV charger on the truck bay wall.
Low-voltage
Sixteen PoE cameras around the eaves for Scrypted, plus a video doorbell. Nine Wi-Fi access points, including two outdoor ones for the back porch and the stoop. Cat6 drops at every TV and desk, ceiling speakers in the living room, lounge, back porch and outdoor kitchen, and conduit stubs to the outdoor kitchen, stoop, pond and gate. The server room gets its own quiet mini-split so the rack stays cool around the clock.
Conditioned attic
Spray foam on the underside of the roof deck. The HVAC and water heater sit in an attic near 80° instead of 140°. Add a whole-house dehumidifier tied into the HVAC, and a drain pan with a leak sensor under the water heater.
Porch screens
Still under discussion: motorized retractable screens in the back porch and outdoor kitchen openings, which roll up out of sight. Ceiling fans on every porch either way.
Not taken
No safe room, no pool bath door, no closet island, and no flood-elevation change, since the site sits well above flood elevation.

For the architect and engineer

Beams
A 16 ft header over the living room glass wall, with a 12" pocket wall for the stacked panels and a steel portal or moment frame, since little solid wall is left on that side. A 16 ft header over the bedroom glass wall under the 11 ft plate. A cypress-clad beam at 11 ft where the kitchen opens to the living room, and a beam where the kitchen opens to the dining room. A 16 ft beam at x 106 in the office where the old outside wall comes out. A 21 ft header over the garage door that also carries the bonus room's front wall, a beam and post at the trash corner under the bonus room, and a 9 ft header at the mower bay. A 16 ft beam on the back porch between the posts that frame the glass wall.
Slab
The office's porch strip is raised and insulated to floor level. The primary bath's 3½ ft extension and the filled kitchen notch are new slab. Hold the front water garden at least 3 ft off the kitchen wall behind the gravel strip, and the reshaped back pond at least 5 ft from the house, with waterproofing where they come close.
Bedroom glass
16 ft wide with about 1 ft of wall at each end, so there's no room for a pocket. Spec a three- or four-panel stacker, or a lift-and-slide with one fixed panel. If the wall faces west, use low-SHGC glass and add a canopy. Confirm the orientation before sizing it.
Bonus floor
About 16" deep joists or floor trusses over the 26 to 29 ft garage span, no posts in the garage.
Code items
Makeup air for a range hood over 400 CFM. Fire separation under the bonus room and rated, self-closing garage-to-house doors. Tempered glass in the tub and shower windows, the bedroom glass and the guest foyer sidelight. Egress casements in bedrooms 2 and 3 (bedroom 3's window moves beside the bed, so the headboard doesn't block it). A 36 in guard where the bonus floor meets the open stairwell, and a handrail on both stair runs. A curbless primary shower. A 30" × 36" clear space in front of the server room panels, with nothing stored in it, and an outside disconnect. A code-sized attic access and walkway to the HVAC and water heater. Design-pressure ratings for the glass walls, the garage door and the windows (about 140 to 150 mph in Lafayette Parish). Check whether the pond counts as a pool for barrier rules.

Upstairs options

Over the garage, 12'-8" above the main floor. The stair arrives through the left wall into the middle of the floor, where the ceiling is highest. The walls are 3'-4" knee walls front and back, and the vaulted ceiling peaks about 14 ft up. Windows only where they can exist: the two front wall dormers, the right gable end, and a small window high in the left gable above the great room roof.
