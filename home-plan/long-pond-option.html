A long pond across the back

Nothing requires the pond to be round. The round shape came from the first placeholder sketch, not from any Aquascape rule. This option stretches the pond along the back of the house so the terrace ends at the water. The swim is a 68 ft lap lane instead of 33 ft. From the sofa you see about 1.8 times as much water. The wetland and falls move to the far shore, using the same crescent design you picked.

My takeI'd take it. The pond becomes the reason you're on the terrace instead of something past it, and you get real laps. The trade is modest on cost, about $25k–$40k more overall, because the pumps and setup don't grow with the pond and the deck goes away. The bigger trade is that the pool-barrier rules come up against the back doors.

The plan

Near shore
Cut stone coping runs along the water's edge, so the terrace ends at the pond. A 12-inch-deep sun ledge in front of the back porch takes chaises in the water. Three slab steps go down from it into the lap lane. The deep water stays out in the middle, at least 16 ft from any house wall.
Lap lane
A 5 ft swim zone runs nearly the full length. The 8 ft deep zone sits at the far-left side, under the jump rock.
Far shore
It's the same crescent wetland as rev. C, stretched along the back. The upper tier feeds a 7 ft falls on the living room sight line. The lower tier sheets over a stone lip into the right half of the pond and down a short rill into the lily cove at the right end. The far shore is at least 37% wetland, and 42 Centipedes fit, against 23–34 needed for this volume.
Circulation
The intake bay is in the far-left corner, away from the beach. The pumps sit in a buried vault past the bedroom wing. Water returns at the falls and lip, so it flows across the whole pond, right to left.
Fire pit
It moves across the water to the far-left shore, reached by stepping stones around the beach end. You sit with the falls on one side and look back at the house over the pond.
Beach
It's at the left end, next to the bedroom wing. It's shallow, so it can sit closer to the house.

From the sofa

The dashed red line runs down at 45° from the porch footing. The dig stays outside it everywhere. A structural engineer should confirm that for our clay before the porch and the pond are designed together.

What it takes

Foundation
The house and porch footings set how close the deep water can come. The plan keeps the 5 ft zone at least 16 ft from the walls and outside a 45° line from the footings. With the pond this close, the engineer should design the porch slab and the pond edge together, including drainage away from the slab.
Pool barrier
The water ends up about 7 ft from the porch, so a fence between the house and the pond no longer works. The house wall becomes part of the barrier. Under the pool code the city points to, that usually means alarms or self-closing, self-latching hardware on the doors that open toward the pond. The fence then wraps the rest of the back yard and ties into the house at both ends. Youngsville's ruling is still open.
Noise
The falls ends up 56 ft from bedroom 2's back window, versus 65 ft today. A valve can turn it down at night, and the lip makes a quieter sound than the falls.
Bugs and humidity
The near shore is hard stone and moving water, and the planted edges sit on the far shore. That keeps mosquitoes and damp away from the doors. Fish are still the main mosquito control.
Cost
The pond line goes from about $100k–$160k to $130k–$210k. That's checked against a Texas builder's $130,000 starting price for a 50' × 70' swim pond with wetland and cost guides at $50–$100 per sq ft. The pumps, vault and design don't grow with the size; mostly the dig, liner and stone do. Dropping the deck saves $10k–$20k, and the coping adds a little. The net is about $25k–$40k more than rev. C: about $235k–$416k for the whole landscape, against $210k–$376k.
Sun
North is still unknown. A long east–west pond gets full sun on the water, which is good for swimming. Shade then has to come from trees on the far shore.
